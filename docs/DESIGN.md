# Fool's Kitchen: Game Design

Status: v0.2 (2026-10-23), revised after the [TasteMaker audit](research/TASTEMAKER_AUDIT.md). Game director: Claude. Owner: Jared. "TBD" items need a decision before the phase that uses them.

## 1. Pitch

A low-poly 3D restaurant tycoon game. You're one guy who starts with a hole-in-the-wall shop and builds it into a restaurant chain. Staff and customers run on their own, like the Sims. You design the space, set the menu, hire, and buy your way up the property ladder. Short optional mini-games give bonuses.

- **Inspiration:** TasteMaker (Steam app 1289340), for ideas only. We keep what players love (relaxing, build your own place, it runs itself) and fix what they criticise (shallow content, stuck AI, no long progression). Nothing is copied: no assets, names or UI.
- **Who it's for:** a friend asked Jared for this game. Their complaint about TasteMaker is that eventually there's *nothing left to do*, so long-term goals are the top design priority. Also for Jared's family (his wife is a big Sims fan). **The audience is adults**, so humour can be dry and cheeky; it doesn't need to be kid-safe (D15). Hobby scope. Single player, offline.
- **Clean-room rule:** inspiration only. We work from public information and videos of TasteMaker being played, and we never copy, decompile or reverse-engineer it.
- **Name:** "Fool's Kitchen", possessive: it's your kitchen, and you're the fool cooking in it.
- **No story.** Just the progression.

## 2. Pillars

1. **Watching it run is the fun, and it's readable.** At a glance you can see who's waiting, what's cooking, and why a customer left. Click anyone to see what they're doing and why.
2. **Every tier changes how you play.** Each property tier adds a new decision, not just bigger numbers. (This fixes TasteMaker's top complaint.)
3. **It never breaks.** Agents never get stuck for long. Edits re-check paths. A broken sim is a bug, never the player's fault.
4. **Your place, your style (Sims-style building).** Decorating counts as much as profit: colour palettes, wallpaper, lighting, chandeliers. This is Jared's favourite part, so the build tools get first-class effort.
5. **Mini-games are optional seasoning.** Skipping one never blocks progress. Playing one gives a bonus.

## 3. Core loop

```
Morning (closed): plan menu and prices, buy stock, hire, rebuild
   -> Open: the service day runs on its own; you watch, nudge, assign the owner, play mini-games
      -> Last orders, then close (D2)
         -> Evening report: profit and loss, reviews, XP, unlocks
            -> Morning ...
```

- **Day length:** a service day takes about 5 to 8 minutes real time at 1x. Speeds: pause, 1x, 2x, 4x. Morning and evening are paused menu phases.
- **Meta loop:** reputation and owner level unlock recipes, items, themes and property tiers. Career milestones mark the way.

## 4. Systems

### 4.1 People (auto mode)

**Customers**
- **Lifecycle:** arrive, queue, get seated, order, wait, eat, pay, review, leave. Patience drops while waiting, and they leave angry when it runs out.
- **Needs:** food quality, wait time, price fairness, cleanliness, decor, and restroom (from Tier 2).
- **Types** (they unlock with tiers and themes, and change who comes): Regular, Student (cheap, patient), Family (groups, kid-friendly menu), Foodie (quality-sensitive, tips well), Critic (Tier 5 event). Each type has preferences and a patience and budget profile.
- **Restroom need is family-friendly:** an unmet need means an unhappy guest who leaves early. There is no mess on the floor.

**Staff**
- **Roles:** Cook, Server, Cleaner (Tier 1 to 2). Then Host, Dishwasher and Bartender (Tier 3 to 4), and Manager (Tier 3, runs a site while you're away; D24).
- **Stats:** skill per role (cooking, service, cleaning) from 1 to 5, speed, wage, and mood.
- **Traits** (one or two each, shown at hire): for example Fast, Clumsy, Charming, Tidy, Lazy.
- **Growth:** staff earn XP in the role they work and level up their skill. Better staff ask for raises.
- **Priorities:** a per-staff priority matrix (like the Sims or RimWorld): each role's tasks set to high, normal, low or off.
- **"Why" readout:** click anyone to see their current task, its target, the time on task, and their top need.

**Owner (you)**
- Click-to-assign (D4): pick a station or role, he walks there and works it, using the same task system as staff. Mini-games launch from his station.

**Pets (Tier 2 unlock)**
- **Restaurant cat (#119):** a one-time $40 morning offer from owner level 4; cat food is $2 a day ("Cat food" in the evening report). It wanders the dining room and restroom, naps in sunny spots by a window (by a wall when there is no window) and starts each day asleep on its bed spot. Waiting guests within two cells are 10% more patient, and a waiting guest next to it may pet it once per visit for a happy review. Purely charming: it never blocks anyone, and there are no pests (no pest system yet). Prices and the charm live in `data/balance/cat.tres`.

- **Health inspector (#302):** from owner level 3, on each open day at least a week after the last visit, a 15% chance (a named stream) that an inspector walks in unannounced, takes a look round the dining room and restroom for about a minute with a clipboard (a real person who reserves cells, never orders) and leaves. They read only what the shop already tracks: dirty tables, floor and restroom upkeep spots, food thrown out as spoiled this morning and dirty plates piling up. Weighted points give a grade: A (up to 2), B (up to 7), C. The evening report shows "Health inspection: grade B" with the worst three findings; a grade card hangs in a street window until the next visit (it stays up after the week's nudge ends; a shop with no street window or door shows none); arrivals are x1.05 (A), x1.00 (B) or x0.90 (C) for the next 7 days. No fines and no game over. All numbers live in `data/balance/inspection.tres`.

### 4.2 Kitchen, menu and food
- **Order chain:** a server takes the order and a ticket goes on the board. A cook fetches ingredients from storage, preps (cutting board), and cooks (stove, fryer or oven). The plate goes to the pass, a server delivers it, the guest eats, and the table needs bussing before it can be reused.
- **Plate cycle (Phase 2):** a limited plate stock. Dirty plates go to the sink or dishwasher, and running out of clean plates stalls the kitchen. TasteMaker players liked this as a puzzle but hated it when it broke.
- **Recipe:** ingredients, steps (prep station and time, cook station and time), base quality, standard price, unlock tier and level, and theme tags.
- **Quality** = recipe base, then cook skill, then equipment tier, then ingredient freshness. Each factor is shown in the dish tooltip so you can see why quality is what it is.
- **Pricing:** the player sets a price per dish, shown against the "standard" price. Guests weigh price against quality and their type's budget.
- **Stock:** ingredients have shelf life. There's a stock panel, a low-stock alert, and optional automatic reordering at a small premium.
- **Deliveries (D25):** morning orders arrive at once. An order placed during service comes with the next daytime delivery, which you can watch: a delivery driver walks the crates along the back alley and in through the back door (the front door if there is none) and sets them down inside, and the cleaner (or a server, without a cleaner) carries each crate to the fridge, freezer or shelf. Goods count as stock once put away; anything still waiting at closing is stored, so nothing is ever lost. A shop taking several mid-day orders with no cleaner pays a visible service cost (servers leave guests to carry crates); hiring a cleaner removes it.
- **Carry capacity:** one plate by default. Trays (an upgrade) allow 2, then 4. This fixes TasteMaker's "one plate per trip" tedium.
- **Recipe discovery (later):** combine ingredients in a mini-game to unlock variants.

### 4.3 Build mode
- **Grid:** 0.5 m. Walls, floors, doors, windows, tables, chairs, stations, storage, decor.
- **Floors (D25):** every floor cell has a floor type (wide planks, narrow planks, herringbone, small tiles, checker, and later hex tile, terracotta, terrazzo, concrete, carpet) in a main colour and an accent colour from the palette. Laying a type costs $1 to $4 a tile (wide planks are free; replacing a type refunds half its price) and is done in the morning or after closing (Build > Floors: drag a rectangle, Shift-click a room, Alt-click to copy a floor). Recolouring is free at any time in Paint (click: the connected floor of one look in that room, so it stops at room edges; Shift: the whole room). Wide planks, narrow planks and herringbone are the starting set; the rest unlock with owner level. Looks only: floors change nothing but the vibe's floor colour (the colour most of the guests' floor has).
- **Doors (D25):** the street door is the only guest door. A shop may have one back door, one cell wide, on a back or side wall, for staff and deliveries; like a service door it swings out, flat against the outside wall, as people pass.
- **Footprints show working tiles** (where a worker stands), so layouts are readable before you place them.
- **Live path check:** unreachable tables or stations are highlighted as you build. It warns but doesn't block. Nobody walks through a seat, so chairs, stools and booths that wall something off warn while you place them (#315); a seat in the doorway is refused.
- **Blueprints:** save a group of items (for example a 4-seat table cluster or a fry station) and stamp it again. This answers TasteMaker's "space management" pain.
- **When you can edit:** walls, rooms and stations can only be changed while closed (morning or evening). Decor and furniture can move while open, and every edit triggers a path re-check and agent re-plan.
- **Land:** buy adjacent plots to expand. A second floor is a Tier 4 upgrade.

### 4.4 Decor and themes
- **Colour everywhere:** walls, floors and most furniture have paint and material slots, chosen from curated palette swatches (Sims-style). Recolouring is free, so it encourages experimenting.
- **Lighting as decor:** lamps, sconces and chandeliers (Tier 3+) add ambience and change the room's light.
- **Ambience per seat:** decor within a radius of each seat. Each item type has diminishing returns (the 1st candle counts fully, the 5th barely counts), and **variety earns a bonus**. This stops TasteMaker's candle-stacking exploit.
- **Negative items:** a mop or bin next to a table subtracts ambience.
- **Theme score:** the share of placed items tagged with a theme. Above the threshold, the restaurant counts as that theme (Diner, Italian, Sushi, Fine Dining, and more later).
- **Theme effects:** which customer types come, how much price they accept, and a bonus for on-theme dishes.

### 4.4b Outside look (D32)
Every street is a "cutesy but fun" frame around the shop (Jared, 2026-11-11): it should make you smile, like a toy town, never a realistic city. Not playable yet (patio and rivals come later, #168, #173).
- **Grounded, never floating:** everything sits on real ground (meadow, lawns, verges, kerbs), and every building and prop stands on a baked shadow. The world ends in a deliberate edge (a tree line), and the camera can never see past it.
- **Chunky and warm:** rounded, low-poly shapes at person scale; warm, soft palette from the shared swatches (gentle pastel roofs, so the player's shop is the strongest colour); playful details (awnings, flower boxes, birds, swaying signs, cosy back gardens, a toy shop and a pet shop) over realism. Lively, never cluttered: props stay off walking lanes, doors and the lot.
- **Day and night:** street lamps glow at night and so do the neighbours' windows, a few going dark late (#440).
- **A beach behind the shop (D37, #588):** a preset may name a shore (`ShoreSkin`). Behind the back lane the meadow then gives way to dunes, a plank path, a lively beach and the open sea, which is the world's edge on that side; the view may pan as far over the beach as over the street. Its life (gulls, boats, waders, surf) runs on the street's display clock, and it empties at night.
- **A car-free promenade and a terrace of beach shops (D39, #596):** a preset may name a promenade (`PromenadeSkin`) instead of a road: one wide sweep of paving with a centre band of double-lantern lamp posts, benches and planters, a wave inlaid down it, bollards on the far edge and an ice-cream cart; people stroll, jog, cycle, push prams and walk dogs, and no cars come by. A preset may also name its terrace (`StreetSet.terrace_left` / `terrace_right`): the neighbours, in order outward from the lot, each with a painted sign and its own form (a shop, a hatch kiosk, a beach hut, an arcade front). Each is its own piece (`NeighbourhoodDressing.neighbour(id)`) the view can show trading or closed and for sale (awning rolled up, windows whitewashed or the shutter down, an agent's board out front), ready to be bought (#597). The terrace stands wall to wall with the lot (#600): the next-door shops back onto the maximum lot's edge with blank party walls, the shops running on from them get walled back yards to the lot's back line, the lot and those yards are one paved block instead of lawn, and each of the lot's unbuilt side strips meets the street with a wall and gate just outside the lot (dropped where the player builds up to the frontage). Nothing ever stands on the lot itself. A closed arcade's bulbs and lit name go dark.
- **A woodland clearing on a forest track (D39, #607):** a preset may name a forest (`ForestSkin`) instead of a street: no road, cars, pavements or neighbouring buildings. The shop stands in a clearing ringed by tall pines; a dirt track winds past the front and off into the trees both ways, with hikers (backpacks) and cyclists on it. The yard has a timber deck with rocking chairs, a woodpile and chopping block, and a fire pit with log benches under string lights; across the track a glade runs down to a stream with a footbridge on a side trail, deer graze and rabbits hop at the trees' edge, and ferns, toadstools, mossy rocks and fallen logs fill the undergrowth. At night the fire burns, the bulbs glow and fireflies drift. **It grows by clearing woods:** the trees beside and behind the lot are named stands (`NeighbourhoodDressing.stand_ids()`, `set_stand_state(id, FOREST | STUMPS | CLEARED)`, named by compass), always outside the lot's maximum footprint, that a growth step (#597) can show felled to stumps and then cleared. The lot's own land outside its first lease is wooded too (#611): land stands (`east_land`, `north_land`, ...) stand right up to the leased land, never on a cell the player can build on or over the door's approach, and are felled flush (stumps at ground level) once the lease covers them. The cabin's walls default to the Log finish (`StreetPreset.wall_style`, the shop's default wall finish on that street; Build can still change any face), and the porch deck runs back over the entrance row to the facade (drawn only; the grid is unchanged).
- **An old watermill on a country lane (D39, #613):** a preset may name a country lane (`CountrySkin`) instead of a street: no road, town traffic, pavements or neighbouring shops. Old Mill Street (`data/streets/country.tres`) is an old watermill bakery on a narrow lane with a grass strip down its middle, between hedgerows; a slow tractor towing hay bales and a cyclist come along it now and then. A gravel apron and a cottage-garden border lie behind a low dry-stone wall, with lanterns at its gap, an old millstone, a bench and a cart of flour sacks. The mill stream runs past the shop's side, out of the mill pond over a little weir, under a turning waterwheel in a stone wheel pit fed by a wooden trough, and under the lane's stone bridge. Beside the shop a wildflower meadow runs down to a farm gate with a produce stand on the verge (crates of veg and apples, a chalkboard, an honesty box, for #604); a cornfield stands behind. Beyond lies a patchwork of pasture and crops between hedgerows and dry-stone walls, with oaks, willows by the water, far cottages, grazing sheep and swallows. At night the lanterns, the stand and the cottages' windows glow. **It grows into the meadow, then the cornfield:** the plots beside and behind the lot are named land plots (`NeighbourhoodDressing.plot_ids()`, `plot_crop(id)`, `set_plot_state(id, GROWN | MOWN | CLEARED)`, named by compass), always outside the lot's maximum footprint, that a growth step (#597) can show mown and then cleared. The lot's own land outside its first lease grows the same crops (`east_land` meadow, `north_land` corn), never on a cell the player can build on or over the door's approach, and lies mown flat once the lease covers it. The mill's walls default to the Stone and timber finish (rubble stone below an oak sill beam, upright boards above).

The shop stands on the roof of the tallest tower in town, inside a parapet with a rail; the roof runs a few metres past the lot's maximum footprint on every side, so the pan limits (#424) always frame the parapet with the city beyond it. The shop's walls default to the Concrete panel finish (#618): stack-bonded precast panels with dark reveals, lit chamfers and form-tie holes. It grows down only (#597): no floor art yet.
- **A cliff-top café (D39, #380):** a preset may name a cliff top (`CliffSkin`) instead of a street: no road, cars, pavements, neighbouring shops or passers-by. The Seaside Cliff (`data/streets/cliff.tres`) is a white cottage café on a grassy cliff top. Behind the lot and along its side the land ends at a sheer white chalk cliff (Jared's pick), always outside the maximum lot (at least 2 m off it): faint grey bands and thin dark flint lines, its beds stepping in and out a little in lit ledges (some grassy, with sea pinks) and overhangs under a green turf cap, its steep faces shaded as if tipped toward the sky so the chalk reads white by day while faces turned from the light stay darker, and catching the moon with a cool tint at night; pale chalk boulders lie at its foot where the surf breaks, a narrow zawn cuts into the top west of the café, and a little cove with a sandy beach has three sea stacks off its mouth, so whichever way the view turns some face of the drop looks back at it. The sea lies 10 m below (lowered from 12.5 m so it shows in the default views) (`sea.gdshader`, the beach's water, its pattern drawn smaller so it reads as far down); its UV is the distance from the cliff's waterline, so the surf follows the foot and the cove. The cliff casts its shadow on the water, and the lower rock fades into the air. A timber rail on white posts runs along the edge round the lot and the cove's top, with a lifebuoy and a bench; festoon lights hang along it by the café. Guests come up the cliff path: a winding gravel footpath from a small car park and bus stop far off, past lanterns at the door, over a stile in a dry-stone wall, by a finger post where the coast path turns off along the top (guests still appear at the entrance row; the path is drawn). Wind-bent grass, sea pinks and gorse grow on the top, a windswept thorn leans by the stile and sheep graze the fields inland. A lighthouse with its keeper's cottage stands on a headland across the bay, a fishing boat works the water below and gulls glide on the updraft level with the top. At night the lighthouse beam sweeps round, the lanterns, festoon lights, boat's lamp and cottage windows glow and their light shimmers on a moonlit sea. Seating is all outside on the terrace, which is the player's to build on the lot (#380's play rules come later); the rain cover is a made pergola in two looks (`assets/made/pergola/`, art only so far). The shop's walls default to the new Whitewash finish: lime wash over rubble stone courses above a tarred plinth.
- **One recipe per street:** every street is one street preset (`data/streets/<id>.tres`, #425), built by the same code, so each new site gets the same polish with its own character. A new street is new data, never new code:
  - the ground: colours and planting (meadow, sand, verges, trees);
  - the building set: neighbour businesses and houses, their roof shapes, heights and fences;
  - the palette: walls, roofs, doors, awnings, hedges, flowers, night window glow;
  - the props: which street furniture stands on the pavement, and how much small life.
  - Each lot names its preset (downtown, seaside, suburb); the home lot and a lot without one stand on the starter street. The second street is the seaside seafood shack (Jared, 2026-11-11): boardwalk, blues, rope, shells.
  - A preset keeps the shared rules above: same person scale, chunky shapes, everything grounded, and the player's shop always the strongest colour on the street, even on a bright street (Jared). It builds on the shared base swatches and may add a few accent colours of its own, such as seaside blues and rope tan (Jared).
- **Cheap to draw:** batched, view-only meshes. A new street must keep the visual capture within about 15% of the starter street.

### 4.5 Economy and progression
- **Income:** meals and tips. **Costs:** ingredients, wages, rent or mortgage, utilities, upgrades, and loan interest.
- **Evening report:** a profit-and-loss table, best and worst dishes, review quotes, the reasons customers left (with counts), and trend graphs over 7 and 30 days.
- **Reputation:** 0 to 5 stars from a rolling average of the last N reviews. Drives arrival rate and customer types.
- **Owner level (XP):** from profit, happy guests and milestones. Unlocks recipes, items and tiers.
- **Career milestones:** goals that change how you play (for example "serve 50 guests in a day", "reach 3 stars", "run a profitable week with a Foodie theme"). These replace TasteMaker's samey scenarios.
- **Loan rule (D3):** if cash ends the day below 0, the bank covers it with a loan at interest, repaid from daily income. While a loan is open, property and big upgrades are locked. There is no game over.

**Property ladder**

| Tier | Property | Unlock | The new decision it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hole-in-the-wall (rented, tiny) | start | menu and price basics, your first hire |
| 2 | Larger lease on your current shop | level 3, morning deposit | more lot to expand yourself; keep your entire build |
| 3 | Bistro (rent or buy) | level 6 | themes, plate cycle, staff specialisation |
| 4 | Restaurant and piano bar (buy) | level 10 | drinks, evening service with live piano, a second floor |
| 5 | Fine dining (buy) | level 15 | critics, premium ingredients, chandeliers and a dress code |
| 6 | The chain (several sites) | level 20 | managers run other sites in a simplified background sim |

- **Owning vs renting:** buying costs more up front, removes rent, and allows structural upgrades and a second floor.
- **Moving up:** you can keep an old property or sell it.
- **Second location (the friend's request):** unlocks at Tier 3 and needs a hired Manager for the site you leave (D24, #164). Buy a new lot (size, street, rent and guest mix differ) and design it from a bare shell. Switch between sites in the morning. The one you're not at runs in the simplified background sim, and both feed the same bank account. Tier 6 grows this into a chain with managers.
- **No running out of things to do:** the late game has no end state. A chain to grow, rotating weekly challenges, a collection of every recipe, item and theme, and sandbox mode.

### 4.5b Early game: the opening week (#494) and Week 2 (#514)
The starter makes about $50 to $70 a day from $300, and the first expansion only pays when tables and staff grow together (D16). The opening week teaches that, and pays for it. Week 2 then walks the player through Tier 2, so the second week isn't empty.
- **Paid goal chains, one after another** (`data/balance/goal_chains.tres`: `opening_week.tres`, then `week_two.tres`). Each goal pays cash and a little XP the moment it is met (goals about building, buying or hiring are checked at opening, so a purchase undone first pays nothing), as its own ledger line ("Week goals" in the report), with a toast and a log line. The XP joins that evening's report.
- **Opening week, in order** (about $600 over days 1 to 5): change a dish's price; place a decor item; serve 15 guests in a day; put a drink on the menu (a drinks shelf); work the floor as the owner during service; **grow together**: on the same morning add a table (any item in the Table category) and hire a server, both still there at opening; serve 25 guests in a day; get five 5-star reviews in one day (a stretch that rewards good service; a passive start already sits above 3.5 stars, and 4 stars was out of reach in week 1).
- **Week 2, in order** (about $700): take the bigger lease (Tier 2) $150; lay 6 new floor tiles $60; place a four-seat or round table $60; put a fried dish on the menu (needs a fryer) $80; hire a cleaner $60; seat a group of 2 or more together $60; serve 30 guests in a day $100; a calm 3.5-star day: end a day at 3.5+ stars with no guest giving up after waiting too long $130 (Jared lowered serving from 35 guests, and moved the calm day from "nobody turned away for want of a table", which the probe player never reached in 14 days: arrivals far outrun seats in Week 2).
  - *Checked at opening:* the lease is the bigger one; the cleaner is on the team; a fried dish is on the live menu. "6 new floor tiles" and the table count bought floor cells and four-seat or round tables above the count at the last opening (same site, like decor), so a buy undone or sold first pays nothing and nothing laid before Week 2 counts twice. The tiles must come between one opening and the next (a 2x3 patch costs about $90).
  - *Seat a group:* a seated party of two or more that pays. Pairs walk in from level 5 today (level 2 after #511); a booked party (level 2) counts too, so the goal is reachable either way.
  - *Turned away:* guests who left with "No table available" (`no_table`) that day, at most 2; other reasons to leave don't count. The rating is the HUD's one-decimal figure at the close.
- **Shown three at a time**, in chain order, in a card that leads Goals (titled "Opening week", then "Week 2") and in a small tracker under the top bar. A goal met before it shows still pays.
- **Never fails, never repeats:** goals carry over between days; each pays once. A chain ends when every goal is done or after its seventh day (Opening week: the morning after day 7). The next chain starts the morning after, and runs seven days from its start (days 8 to 14 when the Opening week runs its full week; earlier if it was finished early). After Week 2 the usual career goals carry on. No randomness.
- **Saves:** new career games get both; a save from before the chains joins the Opening week if still on day 7 or earlier, and Week 2 if on days 8 to 14 (for the days that remain); Creative has none.

### 4.7 Events (Phase 5)
Rush hour, health inspector, critic, broken equipment, a viral review, a power cut.

### 4.7b Service events (#495)
Small choice cards during service, from day 1 (Jared, 2026-11-14), so a day is never seven minutes of watching.
- **Rate:** one or two cards every open service day, at shop-clock times spread through service: none in the first 45 or last 60 minutes, none within 20 minutes of a rush, a booked party or the critic's arrival, and two cards on one day come at least 90 minutes apart. A closed day has none.
- **The card pauses the game** until the player picks (a click or the 1, 2 or 3 key; Escape does not dismiss it), then the clock returns to its earlier speed. A card due in photo mode leaves photo mode first. Each card has a title, one line of dry, cheeky humour (adults, D15) and two or three choices, each with a short preview of what it does. A choice that costs more cash than the till holds (never a loan), whose stock would not fit its storage, or whose stock is not on the shelves is greyed out with the reason; every card keeps a choice that costs nothing, so the player can always pick.
- **Small, clear, never ruinous.** Every effect goes through a system the shop already has: cash (its own "Service events" ledger line, at most $60), one review in the rolling window, the patience of guests in the shop now (a cut never sends anyone away on the spot), today's later guests' patience or arrivals (at most 20%), tomorrow's arrivals at that shop (the food blogger's buzz: its own nudge, named in the Today log, never curb appeal), a discounted stock purchase (one crate at its real price; a bad batch goes straight in the bin) or a little stock used (counted as food cost), a worker's mood (kept between 1 and 3, so nobody quits over a card), or a quick clean of the floor. A card's review counts with the day's reviews. A "risk" is a seeded roll, so the same run always gets the same outcome.
- **Variety:** a card does not come back within its cooldown (3 to 8 days) and some need something: staff, a named regular, rain, a menu with bought ingredients, or Tier 2.
- **Starter pool (15, plus one follow-up):** off-menu request, a van with no name (supplier), busker, spilled drink, food blogger, the lights flicker, a regular's birthday, the neighbour's complaint, a kid's drawing (a four-star nudge), rain at the door, a critic rumour, and from #506 "Can I leave early?", "Double or nothing", "Flyers for hire" and "A guest with opinions". Each card and choice is in the Today log and the evening report.
- **Sharper cards (#506, Jared, 2026-11-14).** Some cards carry real trade-offs, in four kinds, still inside the same clamps (cash at most $60 either way, nudges at most 20%):
  - **Gamble:** double or nothing ("Double or nothing": the kitchen bets a guest's bill on a five-minute dish, +$25 or -$25 on a seeded 50/50, and the rush costs the other guests a little patience). The roll is seeded by the run, the day and the card, so a reload never rerolls it.
  - **Pay now, gain later:** spend today for more guests on the following days ("Flyers for hire": $40 for +8% arrivals for five days, or $15 for two). All of a day's card nudges together stay within 20%.
  - **Staff vs guest:** side with an upset guest (a refund and a good review, the worker sulks) or with your worker (a one-star review, the worker cheers up) ("A guest with opinions").
  - **Chain events:** a choice can set up a follow-up card days later at the same shop. Seating the food blogger brings "The review is out" three days later (share it for a busy day with a chance of a two-star review, frame it for $8 and a five-star review, or ignore it). A follow-up takes the day's first card slot; if the shop is closed or the owner is elsewhere it waits, and is dropped after 14 days.
- **"Can I leave early?" (#506)** really sends the worker home: they stop taking new work, hand a task they have not started back to the team (or finish one already under way, so no order, plate or dish is left half done), walk out the front door, and are off for the rest of the day, still paid a full day's wage. They are back at their post the next morning, one mood happier; "Not today" costs a mood point. The card only names a worker someone else covers: a cleaner (the servers mop), or a cook or server with a second cook or server on the team. The last cook or server is never asked about, and if cover is gone when the card shows, "Off you go" is greyed out ("Nobody can cover for ..."). The owner does not count as cover.
- **Settings:** "Service events" (on by default). Tools, tests and the balance probe run without them unless they opt in; Creative mode has none.

## 5. Out of scope (for now)
Multiplayer, a story or campaign, scenario mode (career milestones replace it), character creation beyond basic choices, mod support, consoles, AI running inside the game (no API calls at runtime), and toilet humour beyond the restroom need.
